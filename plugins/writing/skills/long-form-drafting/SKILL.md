# Long-form drafting

Read the House voice skill first. Then run these stages. Never mix them.

## Collect

- One claim, not a topic.
- One named reader.
- One real moment the writer actually lived.

No outline. No social posts. If you cannot name the claim in one line, you are not done collecting.

## Dump

Ugly draft, starting from the moment. One island at a time.

Do not polish until the claim is proven and there is one thing to do.

## Shape

Walk away first. Then:

1. Restate the claim in one line.
2. Cut everything that does not prove it.
3. Order it: moment, cost, why it's true, sharper cost, what to do, last line.
4. Title last.

## Prove It

Circle any sentence a person who has never used the product could have written. Put the example or the evidence under it. If you cannot, cut it or soften it.

Imagery is something you can point at. Analogies come last and rarely.

## Article shape

Open on a question the reader is already asking. Ground it in a real moment. Short paragraphs, one idea each. Beat by beat with no transition words.

Know / Feel / Do. Me / You / We. A soft CTA that names the use case.

Write from the author's own earlier sentences when they exist. Do not impersonate a talk or invent a keynote voice.

## Newsletters

A postcard, Perell style: one thought the author is actually thinking, then the link. Not an inbox rewrite. The essay can live on any of their properties; the voice is the same either place.

## Done means

One claim, one reader, one thing to do. Finished prose on the table, not bullets pretending to be a draft. Every claim has a pointed-at example. No invented facts.

Page and CTA copywriting skills are for pages, not the essay.
