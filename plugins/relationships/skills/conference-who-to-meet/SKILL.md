# Conference who-to-meet

For a named conference the author is attending: research publicly, rank whom to meet, draft warm reach-outs and on-site openers, then queue post-trip follow-ups. Draft only until they explicitly say to send (**Draft never send**).

## Inputs

- Conference name, dates, venue
- Official hub URLs (speakers, schedule, partners/exhibitors)
- Author's lane (company, product, who they want to meet)
- People to skip (already know) or prioritize

## Networking bar (Ferrazzi / Never Eat Alone  -  operational, not a book report)

Bake these into ranking and drafts. Do not cite the book unless the author asks.

- **Prep before the badge.** Reach out 1-2 weeks out (top Must first). A conference mention is the hook; the meeting is the goal.
- **Generosity first.** Every enrich step asks: what can the author usefully offer this person (intro, tip, resource, attention)? Put that currency in the draft; skip empty flattery.
- **Hallway > sessions.** Rank for people worth coffee/hallway time. Prefer one primary contact per org when the author wants focus. Don't pack a day of sitting through talks unless a Must is speaking.
- **Strike around the talk.** For speakers: draft a pre-session hello or a before-they-speak catch  -  after a talk the line is useless.
- **Warm intros beat cold.** Prefer a shared org, mutual, or partner path when public evidence supports it. Never invent a mutual.
- **Follow up in 24-48h.** After a meet (or a no-show), draft a same-thread follow-up with one concrete next step. Queue it; don't send until go.
- **Deep over weather.** Openers skip small talk; lead with a specific hook from research.

## Steps

1. Pull the public roster from the official site and schedule: speakers, exhibitors, partners. Note gaps (no full attendee list, unnamed keynotes, missing booth map).
2. Enrich each name from public sources only: role, org, why meet, one honest hook, **what the author can offer them**, source URL. Never invent who is attending.
3. Rank Must / Should / Nice against the author's lane. Cap Must to a workable hallway plan (roughly 5-12 unless they ask for more). Prefer one contact per org when they want focus.
4. Draft short **pre-trip** email reach-outs and matching **on-site** openers (paste-ready). Soft booth / wandering invite when true; do not commit the author to attending any session.
5. If they want emails found, use their connected finder tools and label confidence (found vs generated vs accept-all). Soft first sends for weaker matches.
6. After the trip (or when they log meets): draft **24-48h follow-ups** with one next step each. Stay quiet when nothing on the public roster changed.
7. When the roster is thin, say so and work from what's published plus past speakers/partners  -  never fake a headcount or RSVP.

## Outreach frame (default)

- Soft mention they are presenting on X when true.
- Frame: will be at the conference, at a booth and/or wandering; invite them to stop by or say if they want to connect.
- Lead with the specific hook + offer, not a pitch deck.
- No send, book, connect, or RSVP without explicit go.

## Anti-jobs

- Do not produce the author's own event (run-of-show, vendors, dietary, day-of checklist).
- Do not close sales or own standing relationships outside this trip.
- Do not invent attendees, mutuals, or fake RSVPs.

## Delivery

Lead with the ranked list (Must / Should / Nice), then paste-ready drafts grouped pre-trip / on-site / follow-up. Name the channel (email). Silent when the public roster hasn't moved.
